## What does each related concept actually claim?

### Takeaway
The vault treats the Michelin kitchen as Lauren Tan’s (@poteto) craft reframe of “software factory,” and hangs five operational claims under it: the engineer builds the environment, trust is earned and reversible, an inner code loop is fed by an outer trigger loop, verification is what makes a loop a loop, and domain verifiability is the ceiling. Sampling, the dimmer switch, the pass/expo, and the dark factory are sections of the kitchen page and the source notes, not separate concept pages. There is no page for brigade or stations.

### Inferences
- The kitchen metaphor is a scaling and quality story, not a role chart. The only kitchen roles the pages actually name are home cook (one person), chef/tech lead/CEO (owns ingredients, storage, timing, reputation), executive chef / chief of staff (coordinator that does not cook), and a sampling taster. Pass and expo are the host’s mapping onto “stop hand-carrying orders.”
- “Dark” on these pages means agents merge while she sleeps, not an unmanned factory with no tasting. The dimmer is the host’s correction: some surfaces stay light (environment, sampling, course-correction) while merge itself can go dark.
- The trust ladder is qualitative. The pages never publish rung names or a count. The operational sequence they do state is: stop being the proxy (verification) → make the easy path the right path (constraints) → connect outer context with triggers → allow unattended merge only where the domain is verifiable → drop trust and amend the kitchen when the same shortcut repeats.

## What operational architecture is implied, as opposed to metaphor color?

### Takeaway
The operable system is a human who owns intent, constraints, and sampling; an outer loop of connectors that files work; a coordinator that delegates to sub-agents; an inner loop that edits a narrow codebase; and a verification skill, preferably a deterministic CLI, that must pass before merge. Quality gates are lint and types that make bad states unrepresentable, reproduction on main, fuzzing by verifier agents, and after-the-fact commit review. Unattended merge is allowed only for reversible, programmatically checkable work, and is rolled back when the same shortcut shows up more than once.

### Cited Findings
- Human role. The engineer stops being the “meat proxy” (5:09–5:13) and the person who ferries Slack into the agent (36:07). The job becomes the environment (26:17), expressing intent (7:17), providing context rather than control (41:40), sampling rather than tasting every dish (49:34), and course-correcting the kitchen when a shortcut repeats (50:33). Reputation stays attached to the outcome (14:49). She still reviews commit history after agents merge and will revert, modify, or add a lint (52:45). — [source](https://postgrok.com/sources/2026-10-05-youtube-poteto-software-factory); [environment-and-constraints](https://postgrok.com/concepts/environment-and-constraints)
- Agent roles that are specified, not metaphorical. Inner-loop “agent engineers” write code toward a snapshot of intent (35:32). A Cursor project is a coordinator with its own computer that “doesn’t do the work itself” and delegates to sub-agents (42:52). Verifier agents, up to about ten and tunable down to one, fuzz a PR by driving the real UI (52:50). Grok bots are the outer loop: they watch Slack, X, email, and Linear on routines and message a Cursor project (42:13, 44:03). She describes “more than ten chiefs of staff, each working on a different area” while she sleeps (52:02). Grouping beats one-agent-per-bug so the coordinator can see a shared cause (44:33). — [inner-outer-loop-agents](https://postgrok.com/concepts/inner-outer-loop-agents); [source](https://postgrok.com/sources/2026-10-05-youtube-poteto-software-factory)
- Artifacts named. Skills as markdown process (1:01:12), expected to shrink to workflow and drop script commands (1:03:47). A skill wrapper plus a deterministic CLI (21:45, 22:13). pstack pieces: poteto-mode router, playbooks (23 as of 0.15.0, conditionally loaded), verification skill and `/create-verification-skill`, how, why, unslop, recall, and the Build-the-Lever principle. A buffer document that a scheduled agent appends to, reviewed every couple of days, rather than an immediate fix (47:40). Lint rules, type-system narrowing, feature directories instead of god files, and the internal Electron framework Dune (25:26, 29:22, 30:10, 31:29, 31:46). `program.md` is Karpathy’s artifact, cited as the nearby “lightweight skill,” not hers. — [skills-as-process](https://postgrok.com/concepts/skills-as-process); [pstack](https://postgrok.com/entities/pstack); [source](https://postgrok.com/sources/2026-10-05-youtube-poteto-software-factory); [andrej-karpathy](https://postgrok.com/entities/andrej-karpathy)
- Control loops, in the order the pages state them. (1) Inner loop: code toward a snapshot of intent. (2) Outer loop: connectors and triggers so the snapshot does not go stale; she rejects building a “context graph” (38:22). (3) Verification loop: the agent runs the product, observes, and iterates; without that, “loop” is not a loop (17:50). (4) Autopilot loop: spawn verifiers, fuzz, fix, repeat until the PR can land (52:50). (5) Human sampling loop: morning commit review, plus a multi-day buffer before fixing a class of footguns (52:45, 47:40). (6) Environment loop: each repeated failure becomes a lint, a type, a skill, or a CLI so the next agent stumbles into the rule (31:46, 32:38, 50:33). — [inner-outer-loop-agents](https://postgrok.com/concepts/inner-outer-loop-agents); [agent-verification-skill](https://postgrok.com/concepts/agent-verification-skill); [environment-and-constraints](https://postgrok.com/concepts/environment-and-constraints)
- Quality gates stated as conditions, not as a CI config. Confirm the bug still exists on main, not only in one user’s setup (37:28). Do not accept a passing build; poteto-mode “runs the code to check real behavior” (pstack page). Deterministic checks should be a script an agent runs (“run this” rather than “trust me”). Narrow paths: guardrails such that bad code is hard to represent. Fuzz by driving the app like a user and looking for regressions. Domain gate: if the work cannot be verified programmatically, one-way doors stay one-way and the ladder’s top rung is “very hard” (56:32, 57:38). Two-way door means merge-and-revert is cheap; one-way means data loss or an action that cannot be walked back (56:04). Overnight self-merge is described as something she does, and also as something she will not sell as a button (51:20, 52:02). — [inner-outer-loop-agents](https://postgrok.com/concepts/inner-outer-loop-agents); [pstack](https://postgrok.com/entities/pstack); [verifiability-and-one-way-doors](https://postgrok.com/concepts/verifiability-and-one-way-doors)
- Trust rule. Trust is evidence-based and reversible. Repeated identical shortcuts across agents are the signal to change the environment, not to prompt that one agent harder (trust-ladder page; 50:33). The contrast pages say the failure mode is trusting a greenfield “sort of kind of works” result (one prompt awe) until you “trust AI more than I trust myself.” — [trust-ladder-agents](https://postgrok.com/concepts/trust-ladder-agents); [one-prompt-awe](https://postgrok.com/concepts/one-prompt-awe); [ai-psychosis](https://postgrok.com/concepts/ai-psychosis)
- What she says the architecture is not. Not vibe coding that forgets the code exists (host, 54:49, and she agrees someone still tastes). Not a company brain or context graph (38:22). Not 2,500 features; much of the volume is gardening (46:01). Not “just use pstack” (51:20). Not solved for unverifiable domains (57:38). — [source](https://postgrok.com/sources/2026-10-05-youtube-poteto-software-factory)

### Inferences
- A minimal harness that matches only what these pages specify would have: a channel subscription or MCP as the outer trigger; a queue or buffer document; a coordinator that batches related items and delegates; a repo whose lint and types make a class of mistakes unrepresentable; a verification CLI the agent must run against the real app; a human review of merged history and of the buffer, not of every keystroke; and an explicit hold on changes that cannot be reverted or checked. Pass/expo/dimmer/Michelin stars add no extra mechanism beyond that.
- “Own harness” in the 36:51 line is an alternative to Slack MCP (a subscription into one channel plus an instruction to watch it). It is not a described product architecture. pstack’s “other harnesses” means portability of the same skills off Cursor, not a second control theory.
- The coordinator’s “several agent topologies” are asserted (43:39) but never enumerated. Any topology catalog would be invented.
- Dune is an example of the constraint-driven codebase (feature directories, restrictive lint, hard to write bad code), not a general agent runtime. The pages do not say it schedules agents.

### Gaps

- Cursor projects, Grok Bot routines, and Bend are described only in speech. Their entity pages 404, so product behavior is not independently filed.
- The 2,000 and 2,500 PR figures are self-reported (stream vs her Pt. 1 guide). The vault author already discounts them as gardening. This research did not verify either number against a forge.
- No page specifies how sampling is chosen (rate, risk class, or random vs triggered). “Sampling” remains her word for not tasting every dish.
- Brigade, station, and a written RACI are absent. Mapping sub-agents onto a classical kitchen brigade would be outside the source.
