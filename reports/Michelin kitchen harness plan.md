# Staff the kitchen before the dark

Lauren Tan’s Michelin kitchen is an operating model for quality at volume, and the thing the 2026-10-02 captions call Dune is a closed, in-repo constraint surface inside that model, not a public agent harness and not a second name for the kitchen. The stream to imitate is Matt Pocock’s live interview, YouTube `MN9dGgmLyso`, as filed from auto-captions by Postgrok on 2026-10-05; there is no channel transcript, and **the spelling “Dune” was never checked against a repo, slide, or her own post**. A greenfield harness in this empty workspace should therefore ship two layers in order and then stop: a target repo whose feature directories and lint make a class of bad edits fail closed, and a Grok Responses-API loop (`grok-4.7`, client-side tools) whose outer trigger, buffer, non-implementing coordinator, inner workers, verification CLI, verifier subagents, and human sampling edit the kitchen rather than merge while nobody watches. Unattended merge, a context graph, a public Dune clone, one agent per bug, server-side code execution as the workspace, and a copy of the sibling Harness² recursion optimizer are out of scope.

## Factory is accurate, and still the wrong picture

On 2 October 2026 Matt Pocock interviewed Lauren Tan (@poteto), creator of pstack, under the title “LIVE: Poteto (creator of pstack) on shipping 1,000's of PR's a month at SpaceX.” YouTube oEmbed confirms that title and his channel; the stream date and the “about 1 hour 5 minutes” runtime come from Postgrok’s caption ingest, not from a duration field this research fetched ([YouTube oEmbed](https://www.youtube.com/oembed?url=https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MN9dGgmLyso&format=json), [Postgrok source](https://postgrok.com/sources/2026-10-05-youtube-poteto-software-factory)). At caption clock **11:20** she says she has never really liked “software factory,” not because the phrase is inaccurate, but because people who hear factory do not hear quality or craft. **“Michelin Kitchen is the thing I’ve landed on. It’s much more aspirational.”** ([Michelin kitchen](https://postgrok.com/concepts/michelin-kitchen-metaphor)). A later cut on another channel, not this livestream, adds that the work is creative rather than one product on an assembly line ([Get into AI upload](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GgYWPI0Ou-A)). Do not import that “identical parts” line back into `MN9dGgmLyso`.

The picture that follows is a scaling story, not a brigade chart. Becoming a chef means stopping cooking every dish: the tech lead, “CEO of the kitchen,” orders, stores, and prepares ingredients, and **the name stays on the outcome** (**14:08–14:49**). Ingredients, in the vault’s gloss of that passage, are skills, environment, and codebase. Once one kitchen exists she can leave it; each big project is a restaurant she helicopters between (**34:02**). The pass and the expo are the host’s extension at about **40:10**, not her product name: hand-starting every chat is carrying orders yourself. At **49:34** she refuses to stop tasting, and she also refuses to taste every dish across restaurants, so review becomes sampling. At **50:01** she grants the factory image for that one job, a quality supervisor who samples a line. That concession does not retract 11:20. At **50:33** the course correction lands on the kitchen (skills, constraints, lints, types) when many agents share a shortcut, not on the single cook. No page in the Postgrok cluster uses “brigade” or “stations” ([sitemap](https://postgrok.com/sitemap.xml), [Michelin kitchen](https://postgrok.com/concepts/michelin-kitchen-metaphor)).

The same hour states the job the metaphor is for. At **7:17** the bottleneck is no longer the agent but the ability to express intent. ## Six loops, four roles, and gates that are not a CI config

The operable system on the caption pages is narrower than the metaphor’s color. The human owns intent, the environment, and sampling, and stops being the “meat proxy” who carried Chrome DevTools traces by hand (**5:09–5:41**) or the person who ferries Slack into the agent. Four agent roles are specified rather than sketched as stations. Inner-loop “agent engineers” write code toward a snapshot of intent (**35:32**). A Cursor project is a coordinator with its own computer that **does not do the work**; it delegates, orchestrates, and, on a burst of about thirty related reports, picks a topology that the captions never enumerate (**42:52–43:39**) ([inner and outer loops](https://postgrok.com/concepts/inner-outer-loop-agents)). Verifier agents, tunable from about ten down to one, drive the real UI before a pull request lands (**52:50**). Grok bots are the outer loop: they watch Slack, X, email, and Linear and file work into a Cursor project. Her wiring sentence is explicit: **“Cursor projects are my inner loop and Grok bot is my outer loop”** (**44:03**). “More than ten chiefs of staff” (**52:02**) is her headcount image for that fleet while she sleeps, not a published org chart.

Six loops fall out of those roles, in the order the pages state them. The inner loop implements against a snapshot that goes stale. The outer loop is a trigger, a Slack MCP or “your own harness with a subscription into one channel,” that reproduces a bug with the verification skills, confirms it still exists on main, and files it (**36:51, 37:28, 42:13**). She calls a company brain or a context graph unnecessarily complex (**38:22**) ([inner and outer loops](https://postgrok.com/concepts/inner-outer-loop-agents)). The verification loop is what makes a loop a loop: the agent runs the code, uses it as a person would, and takes traces and snapshots, “hands and eyes,” so the human leaves the proxy seat (**16:24–17:50**) ([verification skill](https://postgrok.com/concepts/agent-verification-skill)). The how skill and the unslop skill were good prose and still left her as the one who noticed failure. The autopilot loop spawns those verifiers, fuzzes by clicking, fixes, and only then lands; she calls it token-heavy. The human sampling loop reads commit history after land and reverts, modifies, or adds a lint (**52:45**), and it also uses a buffer: a scheduled scan appends React footguns to a document she reads every couple of days, because fixing each smell immediately misses the shared cause (**47:40–48:22**). The environment loop is the failure-to-constraint rule at **31:46**: every observed mistake becomes a lint rule or a structural impossibility. The host’s “stumbles into the rules” line at **32:38** is his restatement, not hers.

Artifacts match that split between judgment and mechanism. A skill is process in markdown and should shrink toward workflow as models improve; last year’s skills spelled exact commands that newer models do not need (**1:01:12, 1:03:47**) ([skills as process](https://postgrok.com/concepts/skills-as-process)). The deterministic core does not stay in the prose. She extracts it into a small CLI that is “glue” over Playwright and the Chrome DevTools Protocol, because otherwise every agent rebuilds verification, and she sends mechanical migrations through AST codemods (**21:08–23:42**). pstack is the productized skill stack (poteto-mode, playbooks, recall, a verification skill), public at the Cursor plugin tree and installable as a Grok Bot plugin; it is not named Dune ([pstack entity](https://postgrok.com/entities/pstack), [pstack README](https://github.com/poteto/plugins/blob/main/pstack/README.md)). Every Cursor or xAI app she mentions carries an auto-maintained verification skill (**19:10**). The **~2,500 production pull requests** she cites on this stream are not 2,500 features; much of the volume is gardening, including work that comes from her still reading code (**46:01**). Postgrok’s entity page contrasts that figure with about 2,000 in her own 31 August 2026 guide. No independent forge inventory was found ([Lauren Tan](https://postgrok.com/entities/lauren-tan)).

Quality gates in the captions are conditions, not a named CI vendor. Confirm the bug on main. Do not accept a passing build in place of using the app. Keep paths narrow: a good codebase is easy to change because guardrails constrain the agent and the human to narrow paths (**25:26**), and she maps that to TypeScript type narrowing (**29:22**). One-way doors, the host’s challenge at **56:04**, stay one-way where the work cannot be checked; where it can, she says they become two-way (**56:32**). She names Bend as a language that marries programs with proofs, against a separate proof in Lean or TLA+, and she admits she has no general answer for medicine, law, and finance (**57:38–57:56**) ([verifiability](https://postgrok.com/concepts/verifiability-and-one-way-doors)). Overnight self-merge is something she describes doing, after the first night scared her, and something she reviews in the morning. It is not a day-one requirement.

## She keeps the dark night and refuses both poles around it

The October conversation sits in a 2026 argument about whether humans still read code, even where she does not footnote it. Dan Shapiro’s 23 January 2026 essay maps coding automation onto driving levels: most “AI-native” work at level 2, the diff-manager at level 3, spec-and-skill authors who leave for twelve hours and then check tests at level 4, and a level 5 black box, the Fanuc dark factory, “where humans are neither needed nor welcome” ([Shapiro](https://www.danshapiro.com/blog/2026/01/the-five-levels-from-spicy-autocomplete-to-the-software-factory/)). StrongDM’s 6 February 2026 factory text, as quoted by Simon Willison on 7 February, defines non-interactive development in which specs and scenarios drive agents that write code and converge without human review, under two rules: humans do not write the code, and humans do not review it. The spend heuristic is at least **$1,000 of tokens per engineer per day**. Scenarios are meant to live outside the repo, like a holdout set, because in-repo tests can be rewritten to match the code; success is satisfaction across trajectories, not a boolean suite. Attractor is the non-interactive agent spec and CXDB the context store ([Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Feb/7/software-factory/), [factory.strongdm.ai](https://factory.strongdm.ai)). Tan keeps their night shift. At **52:02** “it’s become dark” means her agents merge while she sleeps. She refuses their quality story. She still samples, and the host’s dimmer at **54:49** is the better analogy: some surfaces stay light. That places her nearer Shapiro’s level 4 than level 5. Whether she has read either text is not stated. The dialogue is thematic.

The other pole is vibe coding, and it is the host who draws it. Andrej Karpathy’s 2 February 2025 post defines the practice as giving in to the vibes, forgetting the code exists, always accepting diffs, and not reading them, “not too bad for throwaway weekend projects” ([Karpathy](https://x.com/karpathy/status/1886192184808149383)). Pocock says Tan’s method is not that: code and environment are essential, garbage in, garbage out. She agrees a restaurateur still tastes. Her own Karpathy citation in the hour is different: hill climbing with a rubric, and his March 2026 `autoresearch`, where the human still owns `program.md`, the reward, and the boundaries ([autoresearch](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch)). Both vibe coding and her sampling can skip reading every line. They differ on whether the code is allowed to leave comprehension. One-prompt awe, the vault’s contrast cluster, is the failure mode of trusting a greenfield result that only sort of works ([one-prompt awe](https://postgrok.com/concepts/one-prompt-awe)).

Caption versus primary has to stay visible in any build that claims her authority. **Caption-derived, and only caption-derived:** the timed quotes, the kitchen roles, both loops, the buffer, the verifier swarm, the 2,500 figure, the failure-to-constraint rule, and the word Dune. Postgrok trimmed false starts and corrected some proper nouns; Barnaby’s clock differs by a few seconds; the raw `.vtt` was not re-downloaded and the video was not re-watched for this report. **Primary, and not a transcript of this livestream:** Karpathy’s vibe-coding post, Shapiro’s levels, the StrongDM factory page as Willison quotes it, Tan’s Pt. 1 and Pt. 2, and the pstack README. **Host, not Tan:** expo, the dimmer, and the one-way-door challenge. **Absent from every source read:** worktrees as her design, a ticket schema, a sampling rate, a lint catalog, a public Dune, a brigade, and any measured defect or revert rate behind the pull-request claim. A secondary recap of her 21 September talk offers a five-place ladder from human review down to “change the soil”; that video’s own description does not match the chapter order, so the Pocock captions win ([recap video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tZbGWRdlkIs)). Generic 2026 harness explainers add a loop, tools, compaction, permission policy, and instruction files that she does not itemize on this stream ([MLJAR](https://mljar.com/blog/ai-agent-harness/), [Vercel](https://vercel.com/i/agent-harness)). Use them as day-one plumbing. Do not attribute them to her.

The trust ladder is a state on the job, not a vibe. A job starts at `proxy`: a human would still be the one who notices failure. Phase 3 promotes it to `verified` only when `scripts/verify` wrote a fresh passing trace. Phase 2 and phase 7 promote the kitchen, not the job, when a repeated failure becomes a constraint. Nothing in the CLI promotes a job to `unattended`. A repeated shortcut writes `trust: dropped` on the sample log and opens an environment edit. That is the reversible part of [trust-ladder-agents](https://postgrok.com/concepts/trust-ladder-agents).

[environment-and-constraints](https://postgrok.com/concepts/environment-and-constraints) also says where the constraint lives. The host’s restatement at 32:38, which is his line, is that the agent should stumble into the rule rather than remember it. So `failures.md` is a human-owned log. The model may append a row. It may not mark a row resolved, and it may not satisfy the row by editing `AGENTS.md` or a skill instead of a lint or a type. The second identical signature is a scaffolded rule in the target repo. A one-off stays a one-off.

[skills-as-process](https://postgrok.com/concepts/skills-as-process) fixes the shape of `skills/verify/SKILL.md`. The file states the workflow: run the CLI, read the trace, fix, repeat, stop when the trace is fresh and green or the turn cap hits. It does not paste shell flags. `scripts/verify` is the knife. A later `skills/recall/SKILL.md` can mine session transcripts the way her pstack recall skill did. It is not part of phases 1–7. The second time a human writes the same correction, phase 7 extracts a skill or a lint, which is also the vault’s own rule on [how-this-garden-is-organized](https://postgrok.com/concepts/how-this-garden-is-organized).

[verifiability-and-one-way-doors](https://postgrok.com/concepts/verifiability-and-one-way-doors) adds a field the earlier phase table left implicit. Every snapshot names `door: two-way` or `door: one-way`, plus the check that would make a one-way door reversible. Missing that check, the coordinator may still group and the worker may still edit, and the merge helper refuses. Software being “quite verifiable” is her claim about her apps. It is not a default this harness may assume.

## Build a constraint surface, then a kitchen that still has a taster

The workspace `/Users/chamindawijayasundara/Documents/learning_102/grok_bot_harness_v1.0` has research notes and this report and no implementation. The sibling `google_harness_2_v1.0` is a Python 3.12 LangGraph recursion over INV, DOM, and COMPOSE edits, scored by an offline evaluator, with Fireworks and a Pi worker ([proposal](file:///Users/chamindawijayasundara/Documents/learning_102/google_harness_2_v1.0/docs/harness2-deepagents-proposal.md)). Copy none of that control loop. The only sibling habits worth keeping are a spend cap before any live call, a grader that cannot see or rewrite the online run, and permissions enforced in code rather than in a prompt. xAI’s October 2026 card makes **`grok-4.7` the flagship for coding and agentic tool use**: 500,000-token context, text and image in, text out, input **$2.00** and output **$6.00** per million tokens below the long-context band, reasoning effort defaulting to high, via `POST https://api.x.ai/v1/responses` ([Grok 4.7](https://docs.x.ai/developers/grok-4-7)). Client functions return to the caller; the client executes them and sends `function_call_output` with `previous_response_id`, and multi-turn clients must pass `reasoning.encrypted_content` back unchanged ([function calling](https://docs.x.ai/developers/tools/function-calling)). Set `prompt_cache_key` to the session id. Server-side `code_interpreter` / `code_execution` runs on the provider and is billed as a tool; it is not the git workspace. There is a model id `grok-4.20-multi-agent-0309` and a product named Grok Build, and no fetched page describes a public multi-agent orchestration API. Subagents are separate Responses sessions with shorter tool lists. Docs still disagree in places: the `grok-4-0709` card is an older price and context, and a chat-completions snippet’s 128-tool ceiling conflicts with the function-calling page’s 350-tool ceiling. Prefer the `grok-4.7` card, call Responses rather than chat completions, and re-check the console tier before a paid run ([rate limits](https://docs.x.ai/developers/rate-limits)).

Layer A is a target repo the harness is allowed to edit, not a framework called Dune and not an Electron port. Limit it to the caption surface. Each feature lives in its own directory. A restrictive lint and the type checker run as a failing command, so a known-bad pattern is unrepresentable rather than discouraged in prose. A human-owned failure log records an agent mistake once; the second time the same mistake appears, the change is a new lint or type constraint, not another paragraph in the system prompt. Do not invent Dune’s directory schema, its rule list, or a runtime. Layer B is the kitchen, and it is the harness. An outer trigger appends work to a buffer. A coordinator session has no write or shell tools; it groups related buffer items and delegates. Inner workers implement toward one written snapshot of intent. A verification CLI is a local deterministic script the worker must run, and the session rejects “done” without its exit code. Verifier subagents are separate sessions that run that same CLI and return a verdict, not a transcript. A human samples merged history and the buffer, and the only automatic product of a repeated failure is a proposed environment edit the human applies. Connectors to Slack, Linear, X, or email are a late optional trigger behind the same buffer. They are not a context graph.

Build in the order below. Each phase is done only when the stop line is true. The target repo in phases 1–3 can be a tiny fixture inside this workspace; the harness must not treat its own package as the only codebase it will ever edit.

Outer connectors, if ever added, are phase 8 and only as a writer into the phase-5 buffer: poll one channel, reproduce by running `scripts/verify` or a repro script, confirm the failure on the default branch, append one buffer row. That is the caption’s “own harness with a subscription,” reduced to a file. It is not a graph of company context. Playwright and the Chrome DevTools Protocol belong only if a later target is actually a UI. The captions’ glue CLI is evidence that verification must be a tool, not evidence that this repository should grow a browser farm. Hill climbing against a rubric can wait until phase 3’s trace is boringly reliable; Karpathy’s overnight experiment loop is a citation, not a milestone. Holdout scenarios in the StrongDM sense are the right future fix if workers start editing the tests to go green: keep one check outside the writable tree. Do not build digital twins, Attractor, or CXDB to get there.

What not to build, because the sources either forbid it or never showed it: a public clone named Dune; any file layout, lint catalog, or Electron/Next.js runtime attributed to that name; a context graph or company brain; one agent per bug; unattended merge, required-check bypass, or a ten-verifier swarm on day one; treating server-side code execution as the workspace; LangGraph INV/DOM recursion, Fireworks, or the Pi editor from the sibling; worktrees as if she had specified them; a classical brigade; instruction files (`AGENTS.md` or otherwise) used as a substitute for lint and types, which her July 2026 post, via Barnaby rather than this livestream, already ranks as the weaker constraint; and any claim that the 2,500 pull requests are a benchmark this harness should hit. The first night she described was scary because the verification and the kitchen already existed. This plan ends while the lights are still on.

## Conclusion

The build that matches the sources is deliberately smaller than the vocabulary around it. Michelin kitchen names who owns craft once many agents cook; Dune, if the captions heard the name, names how one codebase makes bad cooking hard; pstack and a verification CLI name the knives; Grok Bot names the trigger. Collapsing those into a single “Dune harness” or into StrongDM’s unread merge repeats the metaphor error she made at 11:20, granting the factory its accuracy and dropping the craft. The open question she left on the table is the real ceiling for this repository too: if a change cannot be verified or cheaply reverted, the ladder stops, and no amount of coordinator topology invents the missing check.
